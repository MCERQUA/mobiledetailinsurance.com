Arizona Regulatory Notes

What Arizona actually requires

General industry guidance for Arizona mobile detailers — start here, then verify with the Industrial Commission of Arizona, ADEQ, and your city's business-licensing office.

gavel Not Legal Advice

Always verify current requirements with your state's licensing authority. Rules and thresholds change — confirm before you bind.

badge Workers' Compensation

Arizona is one of the strictest workers' comp states — coverage is generally required when a business has any employee, including part-time and certain family-member arrangements. That makes Arizona very different from Florida or Texas, where there are higher employee-count thresholds. Independent contractor carve-outs are possible but tightly scrutinized by the Industrial Commission of Arizona.

directions_car Commercial Auto Minimums

Arizona's statutory minimums are $25K bodily injury per person, $50K per accident, and $15K property damage. These are floor numbers — Phoenix metro traffic, fleet routes, and the possibility of damaging a customer's high-end vehicle in transit usually push detailers toward $300K–$1M combined single limit commercial auto with hired and non-owned auto added.

water_drop ADEQ & MS4 Stormwater Rules

The Arizona Department of Environmental Quality (ADEQ) and Maricopa County MS4 stormwater program restrict where pressure-washing and detailing runoff can drain. Many Phoenix-area municipalities (Phoenix, Scottsdale, Tempe, Chandler, Mesa) require runoff capture or biodegradable detergent use for any commercial parking-lot work. Compliance protects you from local fines that GL won't cover.

local_shipping ADOT Commercial Vehicle Rules

Arizona Department of Transportation requires commercial vehicle registration on vehicles used for business above certain GVWR thresholds. Mobile detailing rigs built on cargo vans typically fall below those thresholds, but trailer-mounted pressure-washing setups can cross the line. Get the registration and commercial auto status right before a roadside check.

store State & City Licensing

Arizona does not require a state-level contractor license for mobile detailing. However, most East Valley and Phoenix-area cities — Phoenix, Mesa, Chandler, Tempe, Gilbert, Scottsdale — require a local business privilege tax license. Confirm with each city you operate in; some require per-jurisdiction registration even if your home base is elsewhere.

Frequently asked by Arizona detailers

Real questions we hear from Phoenix, Tucson, and East Valley detailers — answered by an Arizona-based broker.

help Do I need workers' comp in Arizona if I have one employee?

Yes — Arizona is among the strictest states. Workers' compensation is generally required for any business with one or more employees, including part-time, full-time, and many family-member arrangements. Independent contractors can be carved out if the relationship genuinely meets the IRS and Arizona Industrial Commission tests, but misclassification is a high-frequency claim. Always verify current requirements with the Industrial Commission of Arizona.

help What commercial auto coverage do Phoenix mobile detailers need?

Arizona's statutory minimums are $25K bodily injury per person, $50K per accident, and $15K property damage. That's the floor — and it's almost never enough for a working mobile detailing rig in Phoenix or Tucson traffic. Most commercial detailing fleets carry $300K to $1M combined single limit, plus hired and non-owned auto coverage if employees or 1099s drive their own vehicles to jobsites. We quote Arizona-appropriate limits, not minimums.

help Does Arizona allow waterless / steam detailing — and does insurance change?

Yes — waterless and low-water detailing are popular in Arizona because of water conservation rules, HOA restrictions, and ADEQ stormwater compliance in Maricopa County. From an underwriting perspective, waterless systems can reduce runoff-related liability exposure but introduce different equipment (rechargeable polishers, large microfiber inventory) that needs to be scheduled correctly. The policy structure is the same — we just adjust the equipment and chemical-liability sections to match how you actually operate.

help How does extreme Arizona heat affect my equipment insurance?

Equipment policies generally exclude losses caused by gradual heat damage or wear-and-tear, but they do cover sudden failures triggered by a covered event — fire, theft, vehicle accident, or electrical surge. The bigger heat-related risk in Arizona is lithium battery failure in cordless polishers and rechargeable pressure washers; we recommend scheduled coverage on high-value rechargeable equipment so a sudden electrical failure or thermal event has a clear claim path.
