General Liability Insurance for Mobile Detailing: What It Covers, What It Doesn't, and What It Costs

calendar_today May 21, 2026 · schedule 10 min read · verified_user Written by Detailer Shield Insurance

Every mobile detailer eventually has a claim. A scratched fender from a dropped polisher, a slip on a wet driveway, a stained paver from runoff, a tipped pressure washer that nicks a customer's door. General liability insurance for mobile detailing is what stands between that incident and a business-ending lawsuit. But not all GL policies are written for mobile work — and the difference between a fixed-shop GL policy and a mobile-aware one shows up the day you file your first claim.

This guide walks through exactly what general liability covers for mobile detailers, the exclusions every operator should read before signing, the real-world claim scenarios that drive premium, what a fair monthly cost looks like in 2026, and the add-on lines that turn a thin GL policy into a complete program. It is written for the operator who runs out of a van or trailer and visits customer locations every day — the profile a generic small-business GL quote almost never accounts for.

What general liability actually covers (and what people get wrong)

A commercial general liability policy is built around four coverage parts. Each responds to a different category of third-party loss — meaning loss to someone other than you or your business. Understanding which is which is what separates a confident claim conversation from an expensive surprise.

  • Bodily injury to a third party. A customer slips on water you sprayed onto a driveway and fractures a wrist. The medical bills, lost wages, and pain-and-suffering portion of a settlement fall here.
  • Property damage to a third party. Your extension cord drags across a customer's freshly resealed paver patio and gouges the surface. The repair or replacement cost is property damage.
  • Products-completed operations. A claim arising from work you already finished and left — a wax buildup that traps a contaminant and damages clear coat two weeks later, for example.
  • Personal and advertising injury. Libel, slander, copyright infringement in your social media posts, or wrongful entry. Smaller in detailing, but real.

lightbulb What GL is not

General liability is not commercial property insurance, not commercial auto, not garagekeepers, and not workers' compensation. Many detailers buy a GL policy thinking it covers their van, their tools, and the customer's vehicle while it's in their hands. It covers none of those things. Each is a separate coverage line — and a complete program includes all of them.

The exposures unique to mobile detailing

A fixed-shop detailer operates from one set of premises, with controlled drainage, a known floor surface, posted warnings, and a single insurance footprint. A mobile detailer brings the shop to a different third-party location every job — and that single fact reshapes what general liability insurance for mobile detailing has to handle.

Six exposure categories show up over and over in mobile claim files:

  • Third-party premises every workday. You are an invitee on someone else's property — a driveway, a parking lot, a private garage, a marina dock, an RV pad. Each one has its own slope, surface, hazards, and bystanders.
  • Chemical runoff into customer property. Degreasers, soaps, wax strippers, and coating prep solvents do not stop at the edge of the vehicle. They flow into pavers, drains, lawns, decorative landscaping, and HOA common areas.
  • Mobile equipment damaging customer surfaces. Pressure washer dolly wheels rolling across stamped concrete, dropped extension cords on freshly waxed garage floors, equipment trolleys scuffing custom flooring.
  • Customer vehicle in your care, custody, and control. The moment you start work on the vehicle, it is in your custody — and most general liability policies exclude damage to property you are caring for. This is where pure GL stops and garagekeepers begins.
  • Subcontractors and 1099 helpers. A buddy you bring in for a Saturday rush, an independent installer you sub a ceramic job to, a friend's teenager helping with vacuum work — all are exposure points GL has to either cover or expressly exclude.
  • Mobile = exposure to HOAs and commercial property managers. When the property is managed by a third party, you may be required to name them as additional insured on your COI — a routine endorsement, but one a generic small-business policy may not include by default.

7 real claim scenarios mobile detailers face

These are the patterns that actually generate insurance claims against mobile detailing operators. Match them against your current policy — and the section list of exclusions — before the next job you book.

water_drop Customer slip-and-fall on wet driveway

Frequency: Common · Average claim: $4,000–$22,000

A customer steps onto the wet apron of their own driveway during a rinse phase and falls. Outcomes range from a bruised hip to a fractured wrist or worse. Even a clean, no-litigation settlement runs five figures once medical bills and a few weeks of lost wages are added up — and a litigated bodily-injury claim can run an order of magnitude higher. This is the textbook GL claim.

cable Scratched paint from extension cord drag

Frequency: Very common · Average claim: $800–$3,500

A cord trailing back to a customer's GFCI outlet gets pulled across the rocker panel or rear quarter as you move equipment. The damage isn't obvious until end-of-detail inspection, by which point clear coat is scored. On a daily driver, a single-panel respray; on a luxury or exotic, an immediate dealer-level repair.

science Stamped concrete etched by degreaser

Frequency: Common · Average claim: $1,500–$8,000

A harsh wheel cleaner, all-purpose cleaner, or engine bay degreaser splashes onto colored or stamped concrete and pulls the sealer off in patches. Repair usually means stripping and resealing the entire section to color-match — and on a stamped driveway, that is rarely cheap.

pets Pet exposure to chemicals

Frequency: Rare · Average claim: $500–$15,000

A customer's dog or cat licks a puddle of all-purpose cleaner or steps in a residue of wheel acid. Vet bills, emergency visits, and in the worst cases sustained treatment. Low frequency, but a single incident with sentimental and reputational weight can become an outsized claim and an unrecoverable Google review.

eco HOA / common-area runoff complaint

Frequency: Occasional · Average claim: $2,000–$12,000

Wash water runs from a customer's driveway into the HOA common area, an adjacent landscape bed, or a community drain. The HOA opens a complaint, possibly with a municipal stormwater fine attached. The claim isn't just cleanup — it can include legal correspondence and reputational remediation with the HOA board.

precision_manufacturing Equipment falls onto vehicle

Frequency: Occasional · Average claim: $1,200–$9,000

A pressure washer trolley tips and the wand hits a fender. A vacuum hose cabinet shifts and a metal edge gouges a door. An IR cure light topples onto a hood. Each is a single moment of inattention; each is a paint, dent, and PPF repair package.

garage_home Customer property damage during the detail

Frequency: Common · Average claim: $600–$5,500

A garage door rolls onto your roof rack, a wand recoil dings a wall sconce, a cord catches the corner of a landscape light fixture. Mobile detailers work in tight customer environments — and the small stuff adds up to a five-figure annual claim cost across a busy schedule.

What general liability does NOT cover for mobile detailers

This is the section most operators wish someone had walked them through before their first claim. General liability is a powerful foundation, but its exclusions are the reason GL alone is never a complete mobile detailing insurance program.

report Standard GL exclusions for mobile detailers

Damage to the vehicle you're working on — that's garagekeepers territory. Your own equipment — that's inland marine. Employee injuries — that's workers' comp. Your commercial van or truck — that's commercial auto. Professional service errors with no physical damage — that's E&O. Pollution or chemical release incidents — often excluded; need a contamination buyback endorsement.

Each of these is recoverable through a properly written endorsement or companion policy — but you have to know to ask for them. A generic small-business GL quote will not flag any of them by default, and you will not discover the gap until a claim comes in.

The care, custody, and control trap

The single most common claim denial we see on mobile detailing GL policies is the care, custody, and control (CCC) exclusion. The customer's vehicle, once you start working on it, is property in your custody — and most GL policies plainly exclude property of others in your custody, care, or control. That is what garagekeepers legal liability is built to cover, and it is the single most important add-on to a mobile detailing GL policy.

How much does general liability insurance for mobile detailing cost?

Premium ranges depend on more variables than any one quote can capture. That said, here is what we see clearing market for typical mobile detailers in 2026:

  • Solo mobile operator, basic wash & wax, under $75K revenue: roughly $45–$75/month for $1M/$2M general liability alone.
  • Solo operator offering paint correction or ceramic, $75K–$200K revenue: roughly $70–$120/month.
  • Two-truck operation with one employee, fleet accounts, $200K+ revenue: typically $110–$220/month for GL alone — substantially more once garagekeepers, commercial auto, and workers' comp are added.

Eight factors move premium inside these bands: gross annual revenue, years in business, employees vs. 1099 helpers, services offered (ceramic and PPF push premium up, basic wash keeps it lower), state of operation, prior claim history in the last five years, vehicle count in your fleet, and customer mix (commercial accounts and dealerships often require higher limits and additional insured endorsements).

Standard limits vs. what you actually need

The default limits offered on a small-business GL quote are usually $1,000,000 per occurrence and $2,000,000 aggregate. That is the industry baseline, and it is what 90% of dealerships, commercial accounts, and HOAs require on a certificate of insurance. A $300K or $500K limit is technically available — and usually a mistake, because a single bodily-injury claim from a serious slip-and-fall will exhaust it before defense costs are even tallied.

When a $2M/$4M limit (or a $2M umbrella over $1M/$2M underlying GL) starts making sense:

  • You operate two or more vehicles in your fleet.
  • You apply ceramic coatings or paint protection film (high-ticket, long-tail exposure).
  • You work on exotics, RVs, marine, or any vehicle valued north of $150K.
  • You service dealerships, fleet accounts, or HOA-managed properties that require additional insured.
  • You have employees on payroll (slip-and-fall and operations exposure compounds with headcount).

The 8 underwriting questions every mobile detailer should be ready to answer

The faster you answer these accurately, the faster — and more competitively — your quote comes back. The slower or vaguer the answers, the more an underwriter assumes the worst and prices accordingly.

  1. Gross annual revenue (last 12 months and projected next 12).
  2. Years in business (and any prior detailing experience under another entity).
  3. Employees vs. 1099 helpers (W-2 count drives workers' comp; 1099 status drives additional-insured questions).
  4. Services offered — wash, interior, paint correction, ceramic, PPF, RV, marine, headlight restoration, engine bay, fleet accounts.
  5. States operated in (single-state vs. multi-state changes filings and ratings).
  6. Prior claims in the last 5 years — full disclosure, including no-payouts. Hidden claims are the fastest path to a policy rescission.
  7. Commercial vs. consumer customer mix — what percentage is dealership, fleet, or HOA-managed property vs. private driveway.
  8. Average customer ticket — a $75 wash operation and a $1,500 ceramic operation are different risk profiles.

Add-ons that turn GL into a real mobile detailing policy

General liability is the foundation. These eight add-ons turn it into the actual program a mobile detailer needs to operate without exposure gaps:

  • Garagekeepers legal liability. Covers damage to a customer's vehicle while it's in your care, custody, and control. Non-negotiable for any operator who touches a customer's car.
  • Inland marine on tools and equipment. Covers pressure washers, polishers, vacuums, IR dryers, generators, and the contents of your van if equipment is stolen, lost, or damaged on the move. Detailed in our equipment protection guide.
  • Commercial auto. Your personal auto policy excludes business use. The van is the rolling shop; it needs commercial auto plus a tools-in-vehicle endorsement.
  • Workers' compensation. Required by state law in most states the moment you hire your first W-2 employee — even if it's a part-time helper.
  • Tools & equipment endorsement. A targeted endorsement (sometimes attached to inland marine, sometimes standalone) that covers small-tool theft and damage.
  • Pollution / contamination buyback. Closes the chemical-release exclusion that standard GL invokes for any spill of degreaser, wax stripper, or coating prep solvent.
  • Cyber liability. If you book online, store customer card data, or run an SMS reminder system, a single breach can produce a notification-cost claim larger than most physical losses.
  • Hired & non-owned auto. Picks up coverage when an employee uses a personal vehicle for a work errand (parts run, fuel stop) — and gets in a wreck on the clock.

State-by-state notes for mobile detailers

General liability insurance for mobile detailing is regulated state by state, and the differences are not cosmetic. Workers' compensation thresholds (how many employees trigger mandatory coverage) vary widely. Minimum commercial auto liability limits range from $25K/$50K in some states to $100K/$300K in others. Environmental regulations on chemical runoff are stricter in California, Oregon, and Washington than in most southern states. Some states require an in-state-resident producer of record on your policy; some don't.

How to file a GL claim without losing your shirt

The way you handle the first 24 hours of a claim often matters more than the policy limit on the declarations page. Five rules every mobile detailer should treat as muscle memory:

  1. Document everything on the spot. Wide-angle photos, panel-by-panel close-ups, time-stamped video of the surrounding scene, customer statements in their own words if possible.
  2. Notify your carrier in writing within 24 hours. Most policies carry a duty-to-notify clause; late notice is one of the easiest ways for a carrier to deny otherwise valid coverage.
  3. Do not admit fault on-site. An apology is not an admission, but a written statement accepting responsibility hands a plaintiff's attorney leverage. Express concern, document the facts, and let your carrier's adjuster make the liability call.
  4. Preserve evidence. Hold the involved equipment, the chemical containers, and any disposable applicators until the carrier confirms they don't need them. Don't discard receipts or texts.
  5. Separate the warranty conversation from the insurance conversation. A goodwill re-detail offer is not a claim. Tell the customer clearly when you're shifting from a service conversation to an insurance one — and notify your agent the moment a written demand or attorney letter arrives.

FAQ — general liability insurance for mobile detailing

Do I really need general liability if I'm a one-person operation?

Yes. Solo operators face the same third-party bodily injury and property damage exposures as multi-truck shops. A slip-and-fall on a wet driveway or scratched paint from a dragged cord can produce a five-figure claim regardless of company size. Most commercial accounts, HOAs, dealerships, and fleet clients require a certificate of insurance before booking, and many states or municipalities require GL for business licensing.

How much general liability insurance do I need as a mobile detailer?

The industry standard is $1,000,000 per occurrence with a $2,000,000 aggregate. That is the minimum most dealerships, commercial accounts, and HOAs require on a COI. Solo operators serving only consumer driveways can sometimes start at $500K limits, but if you work on fleet vehicles, exotics, or apply ceramic or PPF, $1M/$2M with a $2M umbrella is the practical floor.

Does general liability cover damage to the car I'm detailing?

Generally no. The vehicle is considered to be in your care, custody, and control while you are working on it, and most general liability policies exclude property of others in your care. You need garagekeepers legal liability to fill that gap. Confirm garagekeepers is on your policy before quoting any high-ticket job.

Is general liability the same as a BOP (Business Owner's Policy)?

No. A BOP bundles GL with commercial property coverage and sometimes business income insurance. For a mobile detailer without a fixed shop, a standalone GL policy paired with inland marine (for tools) and commercial auto is usually a better fit than a BOP. Brick-and-mortar detailing shops benefit more from BOP structure.

Can I be sued personally if I have GL insurance?

Anyone can be named in a lawsuit, but a properly written general liability policy provides defense costs and indemnity up to your policy limits. If you operate as a sole proprietor, plaintiffs can pursue personal assets above your limits. Operating as an LLC plus carrying adequate GL limits — and ideally a commercial umbrella above them — is how mobile detailers protect personal assets.

How fast can I get a certificate of insurance (COI) for a new commercial client?

With an active policy, a COI can usually be issued the same day — often within an hour. If you don't yet have a policy in force, expect 24–72 hours to bind coverage and issue a COI, depending on how quickly underwriting questions are answered. Have your gross revenue, years in business, and services list ready to speed the process.

The takeaway

General liability insurance for mobile detailing is the foundation of a real insurance program — but it is not the whole house. By itself, GL responds to third-party bodily injury, third-party property damage, products-completed operations, and personal & advertising injury. It does not respond to damage to the customer's vehicle, to your own equipment, to your van, to employee injuries, or to many chemical-release events. The fix is not buying more GL; it is buying the right GL alongside garagekeepers, inland marine, commercial auto, workers' comp, and the contamination buyback endorsement. Get those pieces in place, document your work, and a claim becomes a managed event instead of a business-ending crisis.
